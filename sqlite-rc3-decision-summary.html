SQLite rc3 vs MariaDB: decision summary

SQLite Database Integration v3.0.0-rc.3 is much more stable than the old SQLite control in these local diagnostics, but it is not a blanket MariaDB replacement yet. It is closest to parity in browser-visible flows and slowest under high-concurrency WooCommerce writes.

Decision read: good candidate for small/low-write sites after validation; keep MariaDB for high-write or high-concurrency WooCommerce until more hosted production-like evidence closes the throughput gap.

Why the old scatterplot was hard to read

The scatterplot put two different questions on one chart: throughput on the x-axis and p95 latency on the y-axis. Right is more capacity. Down is lower tail latency. The best possible movement is down and right.

The confusing cases are where SQLite moves down and left: lower p95 latency, but less throughput. That is not a clean win. The compressed tables below call that a tradeoff.

Recommendation

  • Use rc3 as a serious candidate for small sites, local-first deployments, demos, dev/test, and low-write sites.
  • Do not make it the default MariaDB replacement for high-write or high-concurrency WooCommerce production traffic based on these measurements.
  • If adopting, gate by workload: browser journeys, checkout/admin writes, request throughput at expected concurrency, write verification, WAL growth, and lock/busy logs.

Scope caveat: these are local macOS diagnostics, not final hosted Linux production measurements. They are useful for directional decision-making and deciding what to validate next.
